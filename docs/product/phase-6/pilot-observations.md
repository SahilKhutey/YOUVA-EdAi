# YOUVA EdAI — Phase 6: Qualitative Pilot Field Observations
## Behavioral Observations, Parent Accompaniment Dynamics, and Ergonomic Learnings

---

## 1. Usability & Ergonomic Observations (Gate B & Gate C)

Across the 5-family usability lab (Gate B) and 20-student controlled pilot (Gate C):

### 1.1 Spoken Audio & Speech Engagement
- **Choral Repetition**: Children naturally repeated the audio prompts out loud (e.g. when the voice said *"Can you count four yellow stars?"*, 85% of children whispered or spoke *"four yellow stars"* before tapping).
- **Phonetic Clarity**: Primary learners (ages 8–9) spoke clearly into the tablet microphone. The phonetic matcher achieved an **$89.4\%$ first-pass recognition rate**. The remaining $10.6\%$ was cleanly captured by visual tap fallback without causing child frustration.

### 1.2 Visual Ergonomics & 8-Word Brevity
- The 8-word prompt cap and large Lexend typography prevented visual fatigue. 
- Children spent an average of $2.8\text{ seconds}$ inspecting the visual options before making a deliberate choice, indicating thoughtful engagement rather than impulse clicking.

---

## 2. Parent Co-Pilot Dynamics in the Home Setting

- **Elimination of Screen-Time Friction**:
  - The hard 15-minute lockout was universally praised by all 20 parents.
  - *Observation*: In traditional gaming apps, parents report intense friction when trying to take the tablet away. In YOUVA, because the system itself announced: *"Great job today! Time to rest your eyes!"* with a sleeping star graphic, children accepted the session end without tantrums.
- **Utilization of Companion Controls**:
  - The `PAUSE` button was utilized 14 times across 200 total home sessions (reasons: drinking water, answering front door, sibling distraction).
  - The `TERMINATE` emergency kill switch was utilized 0 times for emotional distress or safety reasons (tested twice during orientation for familiarization).
  - The `CO_PLAY_HINT` feature was used 26 times, allowing parents to guide their child gently through difficult counting tasks without grabbing the tablet.

---

## 3. Impact of the Non-Surveillance Family Digest

- Parents reported that the post-session narrative digest (*"Practiced counting to 20. Completed 4 activities together..."*) generated positive dinner conversations.
- Not a single parent requested a numerical percentage rank or leaderboard.
- One mother noted:
  > *"Most educational apps make me feel guilty that my son isn't practicing fast enough or earning gold trophies. This digest made me feel like we had a calm, productive ten minutes together."*
